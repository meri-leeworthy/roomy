### 2. A bridge send that times out is dropped permanently and silently — the cursor advances past it, so the message never reaches Roomy

A `send_failed` skip record is written; nothing reads it and nothing retries. Measured today: **14 occurrences** since 00:00Z (15 in the trailing 24 h), **every one addressed to `did:plc:qzie4v7qwnv3mzaflihrf56f`** — the second hot space — at 03:49, 04:23, 05:19, 05:20, 05:57, 06:52, 07:46, 11:59×3, 12:33×3, 12:40Z, plus one `Failed to sync delete` for the same space. - **Why now.** Proposal 1 explains why the timeouts started; this explains why they are *lossy*. Even with the pool fixed, any 20 s hiccup on this path permanently loses a bridged message — the same class as TASK-156 (silent truncation) and TASK-215 (a channel reported "complete" with history missing), both `[done]`, which makes this the remaining hole in the same seam.
- **Scope sketch.** Keep the cursor where it is when the send fails — advance only on success, or record the failure against the message id and re-offer it on the next pass. - **Acceptance.** A forced 20 s timeout during ingest leaves the message recoverable — it is re-sent on the next pass, or appears in a count of un-delivered messages — rather than being indistinguishable from a delivered one. Test: inject a failing `sendEvent` and assert the cursor does not advance past it.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no user-visible break by itself, but it converts every transient timeout into permanent, unattributable data loss.

## Limits of the evidence

- **Proposal 1's causal chain is measured end to end except for one link: I did not observe one of the two hot spaces' queries queue behind the other's.** What is directly measured: the two DIDs' write volumes (6,404 + 3,832 of 10,489 for 24 h; 97.6%), their `hashSpace` values colliding on `space-4` (FNV-1a, recomputed from the module's own algorithm), `space-4`'s backlog being the only one above 12 over 24 h, every >10 s trace naming one of those two spaces, and `sendEvents.write` holding 10.5–21.6 s of the 5–28 s total.
