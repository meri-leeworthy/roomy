## Proposals (ranked)

### 1. The one periodic job that precedes every storage stall is untimed: measure the hourly maintenance tick

- Evidence: the ninth stall ran **2026-11-11T23:12:15.055Z →
  23:12:52.797Z** — 8 statements ≥ 500 ms on `readstate` (6), `space-3` and
  `space-0` (plus `space-1` and `global` in the closing second), max
  **29,191 ms**, every one with `selfMs ≈ totalMs`. It began **4.0 s** after the tick logged
  `[maintenance] purged 2 stale user_thread_activity rows` at
  `23:12:11.009Z`, and ended by releasing every worker within 300 ms of itself.
  Across the 2.4-day `db-slow` window this is the only shape of its kind: three
  multi-worker clusters, all at minute `:12`, one per day, 25 h apart
  (11-09T21:12:13, 11-10T22:12:15, 11-11T23:12:15). Twice in the 7 days
  of maintenance lines retrieved, on stall days, the tick's own line was
  **late** — 11-05T17:12:16.822Z and 11-08T20:12:20.187Z, 6 s and 9 s after its
  usual `:12:10.8`. The tick is
  `setInterval(async () => …, 60 * 60 * 1000)` at
  `packages/appserver/src/appserver.ts:754-763`; it awaits two deletes and logs
  only a row count. Both deletes are unindexed full scans:
  `delete from user_thread_activity where last_active_at < ?`
  (`queries/userActiveThreads.ts:575-579`) and
  `delete from user_thread_backfill where completed_at < ?` (`:594-600`);
  yesterday's record measured the latter at **4,226 ms** on its own.
- Why now: the stall is no longer only slow. On 2026-11-11 it produced the
  process's **only two 5xx since it booted on 2026-11-03** — two `sendEvents`
  writes timed out at 23:12:47.893Z and 23:12:48.868Z
  (`roomy_db_timeouts_total{type="query"}` = 2, `status="500"` = 2 of 343,603
  requests) — and the bridge queued two Discord messages for re-offer at
  23:12:31Z and 23:12:38Z. Nine occurrences so far, one a day, and the two
  candidate causes (our purge vs. the volume underneath) have different owners.
- Scope sketch: time each step of the tick and log it with its start —
  `scope=maintenance, msg="tick"`, `step`, `ms`, `rows`, plus
  `roomy_maintenance_seconds{step}`. Ten lines in the existing callback; no
  change to either delete, no index added. `#625` measures the effect (event
  loop lag, per-worker stall counters) and cannot attribute the cause; this is
  the one in-process suspect it deliberately excludes.
- Acceptance: a test that drives the tick callback with a stubbed read-state DB
  asserts one record per step with its duration and a counter increment; the
  next stall then reads as either a tick step of ~30 s (our SQL — the index fix
  in CF25 follows) or a tick that finished in milliseconds while workers
  blocked (the host — an operator ticket).
- Suggested priority: P2.
- Not already tracked because: `#625` (TASK-481) instruments stalls and
  `#598` (TASK-461) prices statements, but nothing measures the tick that
  precedes every occurrence, and CF25's index change is the remedy *after* the
  attribution, not the attribution.

## Honest limits

The stall-to-tick correlation is strong but confounded, and I want that stated
plainly: the stall period is ~25 h, so it can only ever land on some `:12` tick
— the alignment alone does not prove the tick caused it. What the record adds is
that the tick is the only periodic in-process job, that two occurrences delayed
the tick itself, and that today's stall began 4.0 s after the tick logged; the
tick's duration, which is unmeasured, is the discriminator.

The bundle dating is a bound, not a version. `oauth sign-in failed` is present
and `how many people saw this tile` is absent across all 173 precached assets,
so the build is after 2026-10-27T02:13Z and before 2026-10-28T00:58Z; a
tree-shaken literal could in principle narrow it further than the truth, which
is why two independent markers are quoted. Nothing serves a build id, so the
deployed commit cannot be named — that is TASK-480 / `#626`.

Production figures come from the public `/metrics` endpoint because Mimir
alternates between two processes (headline 5). Client percentiles come from a
