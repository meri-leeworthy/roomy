## Proposals (ranked)

### 1. A deadman's switch on Roomy's own telemetry: twelve hours of production and staging logs, metrics and traces went missing and nothing said so

- **Evidence.** At 2026-11-03T02:09–02:12Z every Roomy log stream stops, within
  2.5 minutes of each other, in both environments and across four independent
  processes: client `roomy-app-lite` last line 02:09:32Z; staging
  `space-worker` 02:11:19Z; staging `appserver` 02:11:57Z; production
  `discord-bridge` 02:12:00Z; production `appserver` 02:12:04Z. Nothing arrives
  again until 14:11:25Z (bridge `Session restored`), 14:11:50Z (client),
  14:12:10Z (production appserver `appserver ready`), 14:13:54Z (`space-worker
  startup`), 14:13:55Z (staging appserver, build `b9b3ff68`). Tempo holds **0**
  spans for 04:00–12:00Z. Mimir has no sample of `roomy_xrpc_requests_total`
  between 02:12Z (233,620) and 14:18Z (329).
  The collector's own death is visible in Mimir: at 02:13Z the series jumps from
  a per-scrape increment of **60** to the cumulative lifetime value **21,158**
  and holds it until the series goes stale — the signature of a fresh collector
  exporting raw counters because its delta state is gone
  (`deploy/alloy/config.alloy`; all four services push to it, `space-worker`'s
  own startup line names `roomy-a483.railway.internal:3100`). So the most likely
  reading is that the **collector** died at 02:12Z and stayed dead for twelve
  hours, taking logs, metrics and traces with it, while the services kept
  running; the 14:11–14:14Z redeploy (which also moved staging to `b9b3ff68` and
  rebuilt the client) restarted everything and restored it.
- **Why now.** For twelve hours there is no way to answer *any* question about
  production — not "is it up", not "how slow", not "who is erroring". That is
  the state this project's only observability lives in, and the failure mode is
  indistinguishable from a normal quiet morning: no series, no error, no page.
  Today it cost the exact window in which CF21's regression presumably ended.
- **Scope sketch.** A deadman's switch that does not depend on the thing it
  watches: a scheduled job (GitHub Actions cron or a Railway cron service) that
  queries Loki/Mimir for "any `service_name="appserver"` line in the last 10
  minutes" and "`up{job="prometheus.scrape.appserver"} == 1`", and opens an issue
  or pings a webhook when either is absent for two consecutive checks. Keep the
  collector's own health out of it — the point is to detect the collector by its
  absence. Optionally add a `watchdog` remote-write series that the collector
  itself stamps, so the check can distinguish "collector dead" from "appserver
  dead".
- **Acceptance.** Kill the collector on staging; within 20 minutes an alert
  arrives naming which of the two signals is missing. A unit test for the
  classifier (absent vs. stale vs. present) is enough on the code side.
- **Suggested priority:** **P1**.
- **Not already tracked because.** CF2 is about series that *exist* being
  unreadable (`increase()` over a delta). Nothing in the queue, and nothing in
  the collector config, detects the *absence* of all series.

### 2. The Rust `space-worker` retries one permanently-failing materialise batch once a minute forever, and the line it emits cannot name the event

- **Evidence.** `space-worker` (staging) has been logging, once a minute without
  a gap since its first start on 2026-10-31T21:01:28Z:

  ```json
  {"ts":"2026-11-03T15:03:56.455Z","level":"warn","scope":"materialise",
   "msg":"batch failed; retrying","space":"did:plc:4uepaw2cimtrcm2zjh2tqzes",
   "idx":-1,"ord":-1,"error":"FOREIGN KEY constraint failed"}
  ```

  **719 such lines in the last 24 h**, all for one space, all with
  `idx:-1, ord:-1` — the cursor has never advanced past the first batch, so the
  space has materialised nothing since 10-31T21:01Z. The retry loop is
  `crates/roomy-space-worker/src/supervisor.rs:367-377`: exponential backoff
  capped at `ERROR_BACKOFF_MAX` = 30 s (`:115`), with repeat logs suppressed for
  `ERROR_REPORT_INTERVAL` = 60 s (`:119`) — hence exactly one line a minute. The
  cursor it reports is `cursor.0/cursor.1` (`:371-372`), which is `(-1,-1)`
  because nothing has ever been applied; the error carries no event id, no
  `$type`, and no room, so the failing record cannot be identified from the log.
- **Why now.** This is the stack that replaces the TypeScript appserver
  (`packages/appserver` is explicitly temporary, the Rust crates are the
  destination). A space that can never materialise is silent today because it is
  staging and because the retry is cheap; in production the same class of
  failure would present as a space that simply does not work, with a once-a-minute
  warn and no terminal state to alert on.
- **Scope sketch.** Classify the failure in the worker: a SQLite constraint
  violation on apply is *not* transient — stop retrying after a small bounded
  number of attempts, record the space in a `poisoned` state on `/health`
  (`health.listening` on `127.0.0.1:8089` already exists), and log the first
  batch's event ids/cursors rather than the pre-batch cursor. `idx:-1` in the
  line is the tell that the batch identity is not being carried into the error
  path.
- **Acceptance.** A unit test that injects a constraint violation and asserts
  (a) retries stop, (b) the health snapshot names the space and the event, (c)
  a transient error (busy/locked) still retries. On staging, the loop for
  `did:plc:4uepaw2cimtrcm2zjh2tqzes` stops and reports instead.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2**.
- **Not already tracked because.** No task names the Rust worker's error
  handling, and the only worker-facing entry in the queue (`TASK-439`, feed
  discovery) is about which spaces a follower follows, not about a batch that
  cannot apply.

## Limits of the evidence

Proposal 1 establishes the blackout's boundaries to the second and shows the
collector was replaced mid-window (the delta→cumulative flip), but it does not
prove which component died first: all four services push their logs to one
collector, so their identical cutoffs are consistent with either "the collector
died" or "every service died". Only the production appserver's uptime argues —
it restarted at 14:12:10Z, twelve hours after the cut. Whether users could reach
`api.roomy.space` during those twelve hours is therefore **unknown**; an
external synthetic check is the missing instrument, and that is what proposal 1
asks for. CF21's before/after comparison uses `count_over_time` ratios and
per-window means over one route (`room.getMessages`); `sendEvents` and
`getActivityFeed` were not re-measured today, and the post-restart hour still
contains the backfill burst, so its 64 ms mean overstates the steady state — the
>100 ms share (7 %) and the raw durations (5–51 ms typical) are the trustworthy
numbers. Proposal 2's reading of the retry path is source-level
(`supervisor.rs:367-377`); I did not run the worker, and I could not reproduce
the constraint failure locally, so "the batch can never succeed" is inferred
from the cursor never advancing in three days of logs, not from a reproduction.
The queue audit is limited by the same stale snapshot as the previous three
runs.
