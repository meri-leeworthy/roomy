## Proposals (ranked)

### 1. Rebuilding a room's activity projection scans every message in the room, and runs fifty times a day on one space

- **Evidence.** From `db-slow` (`packages/appserver/src/db/worker.ts:151`,
  statements slower than 50 ms, which carry `sql` and `selfMs`):
  `with rooms(room_id) as (select value from json_each(?1)), entries as (…)`
  — `rebuildRoomActivitySql` in
  `packages/appserver/src/queries/roomActivityProjection.ts:124` — ran **51**
  times in 24 h for **49,651 ms** of statement time, range 0.9–5.4 s,
  and **every one of the 51 is the same space**,
  `did:plc:qzie4v7qwnv3mzaflihrf56f`. It is the largest single contributor to
  the day's 881 `db-slow` lines (~175 s of statement time in total), and it
  recurs roughly every 40 minutes around the clock.
- **Why now.** The module's own docstring says the projection exists so that a
  board read is O(rooms) instead of O(rows) (`roomActivityProjection.ts:1-20`),
  but the rebuild path is still the O(rows) scan:
  `ENTRIES_BY_ROOM_SQL` (`:102-119`) selects every message-shaped entity in the
  room with four `left join`s, and the CTE then takes `row_number() over
  (partition by room order by ts desc, id desc)` and `group by room, did` over
  the whole thing. Two or three seconds of a space worker, twice an hour, for a
  space whose board then queues behind it. The trigger is ordinary activity —
  `deleteMessage` and `moveMessages` invalidate the room (`:36-52`, `:366`) and
  the next read rebuilds it (`:523-544`).
- **Scope sketch.** The rebuild needs exactly two answers: the newest message by
  `(ts, id)`, and per-author `max(ts)`. Both are available from the indexes that
  already exist — `idx_entities_room_sort` on `(room, sort_idx)`
  (`db/schema-space.sql:82`) and `idx_edges_label_head` on `(label, head)`
  (`:107`) — so the "newest" half can be a `limit 1` seek per room instead of a
  full partition. The author half needs either a per-author index or the
  incremental path the module already has for creates
  (`mergeRoomActivityFromMessageSql`, `:198`), applied to
  deletes as "subtract one author's max" with a fallback rebuild only when that
  author has no other message. If neither can be made flat in room size, bound
  the trigger instead: rebuild lazily on a dirty set swept at most once per
  minute rather than inline on the read.
- **Acceptance.** The repo already has `perf/probe-projections.ts`; extend it to
  assert rebuild time is flat as a room grows from 10³ to 10⁶ messages, and show
  the `with rooms(room_id)` signature gone from `db-slow` on staging over 24 h.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2**.
- **Not already tracked because.** No entry in the queue mentions
  `room_activity`, projections, or slow statements by name. CF22/TASK-461
  measures statement time; this is the one statement it will name.

### 2. A client fetch and the request that served it are two traces that never meet

- **Evidence.** `packages/appserver/src/telemetry/tracing.ts` builds a provider
  with `ParentBasedSampler` (`:20-23`, `:145-165`) but never *extracts* a parent
  from the incoming request; the appserver's root span carries
  `parentSpanId: None` (fetched from Tempo, trace of a `space.roomy.space.sendEvents`
  request at 2026-11-06T07:0xZ). On the client side the Faro SDK mints its own
  `traceID` per fetch (`packages/app-lite`, `event_name=faro.tracing.fetch`),
  and `DirectXrpcClient`'s four `fetch` sites
  (`packages/sdk/src/transport/direct-xrpc.ts:135,179,243,263`) send no
  `traceparent`. A grep of `traceparent|traceParent` across
  `packages/{app-lite,appserver,sdk}/src` returns **one** hit, and it is the
  appserver stamping its *own* `trace_id` onto its log lines
  (`packages/appserver/src/log.ts:121`). Sampled eight client fetch trace ids
  from the last six hours: **none** exists in Tempo.
- **Why now.** The scale of what this hides: over the newest 5,000 client fetch
  events, the **median client-observed duration is 399 ms, p90 1,140 ms, p99
  ~10 s**, while the appserver's own `duration_ms` for `room.getMessages` is
  **~25 ms**. The same 5,000 events contain **1,892** 401s against **288**
  server-logged authentication failures (CF12), and **303** Cloudflare 530s
  against zero logged 5xx. Without a join, neither the latency gap nor the
  missing 1,604 401s can be attributed — including the 2026-11-01 latency
  episode recorded below, which I can bound but not explain.
- **Scope sketch.** Two ends, both small. Client: one header helper in
  `DirectXrpcClient` that emits `00-<32 hex>-<16 hex>-01` and reuses it across
  the transport retries of one call, applied at the four `fetch` sites (a fifth
  site would silently drop correlation, so the helper must be the only place
  headers are built). Server: `propagation.extract` on the incoming `Request`
  in `withSpan`'s entry point, so the existing `ParentBasedSampler` inherits the
  client's decision and the root span gets a parent. No sampling change, no new
  vendor, no metric.
- **Acceptance.** A client fetch span's trace id resolves in Tempo to a trace
  whose root is the XRPC nsid and whose duration is not larger than the client
  span's; the same trace carries the server's `db-slow` and handler spans. Then
  the ~1,600 unattributed 401s become countable by route in one query.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2**.
- **Not already tracked because.** CF14/TASK-448 (`#578`) makes the client span
  *name the method*; CF13/TASK-447 (`#579`) makes telemetry *name the build*.
  Both improve one end of a request in isolation. Neither joins the two, and the
  join is what every "the client is slow but the server says 25 ms" question
  this week has actually needed.

### 3. Both hourly maintenance purges are full scans of the read-state database

- **Evidence.** The maintenance timer runs `purgeStaleThreadActivity` and
  `purgeStaleThreadBackfill` once an hour
  (`packages/appserver/src/appserver.ts:753-766`). Their predicates are
  `where last_active_at < ?` (`queries/userActiveThreads.ts:574-578`) and
  `where completed_at < ?` (`:596-600`). Neither column is the leading column of
  any index: `user_thread_activity` carries `idx_user_thread_activity_user`
  `(user_did, last_active_at desc)` and the v7
  `idx_user_thread_activity_user_space` `(user_did, space_did, last_active_at
  desc)` (`db/readStateSchema.sql:93-103`, `db/worker.ts:290`), and
  `user_thread_backfill` has **no index beyond its primary key**
  `(user_did, space_did)` (`db/readStateSchema.sql:122-127`). Both statements
  therefore read the whole table. Measured on 2026-11-05T17:12: `delete from
  user_thread_activity` **6,050 ms** and `delete from user_thread_backfill`
  **4,594 ms** (`selfMs`), on the global `readstate` worker.
- **Why now.** This is the database every board read and unread count shares.
  The read-state watermarks line at boot reads "163 of **91,167** examined", so
  the DB is order 10⁵–10⁶ rows, which is consistent with a multi-second scan.
  It costs a write-locked interval on the global worker once an hour, every
  hour, forever, and it is one of only two statements in the day that exceed
  4 s.
- **Scope sketch.** Two `create index if not exists` statements —
  `user_thread_activity(last_active_at)` and
  `user_thread_backfill(completed_at)` — declared in the *migration* that owns
  the table, not in `readStateSchema.sql`, following the comment at
  `readStateSchema.sql:96-103` that already explains why a column-dependent
  index cannot live there. Then confirm with `explain query plan` that both
  purges seek instead of scan.
- **Acceptance.** `explain query plan` shows a range scan on the new index for
  both statements, and neither appears in `db-slow` on staging over 24 h.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3**.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue mentions the read-state
  schema, the maintenance timer, or indexes. Deliberately ranked third: the two
  measurements above landed inside a 33-second window (2026-11-05T17:12:13–46)
  in which eight statements across five different workers took 1.2–8.2 s
  simultaneously, including one on a space DB and one on the events DB. That
  makes 6.0 s an upper bound on the purge's cost, not a clean measurement of it
  — the missing-index fact is certain from the schema, the size of the bill is
  not.

## Limits of the evidence

Proposal 1's 51 rebuilds and 49.7 s are counted from `db-slow`, so they are a
floor: a rebuild faster than the 50 ms threshold is invisible to this evidence,
and the 50 ms threshold is the only sampling this log has (CF22).

Proposal 2 establishes the absence of correlation from the source and from eight
sampled trace ids, not from every request. Eight is enough to say the two trees
are disjoint by construction (no `traceparent` is written anywhere in the three
packages), not enough to rule out a proxy that strips one.

Proposal 3's two statements were measured inside a host-wide stall; the missing
indexes are certain, the multi-second cost attributable to them is not. I could
not size either table from here — `91,167` is the count of read-state watermarks
examined at boot, which bounds the read-state DB's order of magnitude, not its
row counts.

The RSS series in Mimir is internally inconsistent and I could not resolve it:
an instant query returns **400 MB** at 2026-11-02T06:00Z while a range query over
the same series returns **1,045 MB** at 2026-11-02T07:05Z, and a 30-minute-step
range returns **402 MB** at 2026-11-02T07:30Z. Both range reads and the live
`/metrics` agree on today's value, which is why CF23 quotes `/metrics` and the
recent hourly series only. Any chart built from this series before 2026-11-03
should be re-derived before it is trusted.

Production has been up 2 d 17 h. Every "since the boot" figure above therefore
describes that process only; the 2026-10-30 process is the one that carried the
latency plateau, and its logs ended with it.
