## Proposals (ranked)

### 1. Every unknown `spaceId` creates a 372 KB database on the production volume and nothing ever deletes it

- **Evidence.** `getMetadata` opens the space DB before it establishes that the space exists: `const db = openSpaceDb(spaceId)` (`handlers/space.roomy.space.getMetadata.ts:109`), and only afterwards reads `comp_space` and throws `404 Space not found` (`:160`). `getSpaceSummary` has the same order (`handlers/space.roomy.space.getSpaceSummary.ts:35` → 404 at `:56`). Opening a space DB that has no file creates one: `new Database(path, { create: true })` (`db/worker.ts:587`), then `initializeSpaceSchema` execs the **whole 19,959-byte `schema-space.sql`** for a fresh DB (`db/worker.ts:351`). The only deletion in the worker is `deleteSpaceDbFile` on a schema-init *failure* (`db/worker.ts:464`); there is **no sweep of `spacesDir` anywhere** in `packages/appserver/src` outside tests (`readdir` grep → 0 hits).
  **Measured, this run:** 200 cold unknown-space opens took **17,665 ms — 88.3 ms each** — and left a **372,736-byte** file each (throwaway `bun:sqlite` script reproducing `worker.ts:587` + `:351`; not in the diff). Against production, a fresh unknown DID costs `getMetadata` ~0.19 s end-to-end against ~0.15 s for a cached real space.
  **Production has already done this at scale.** `Space not found` on 2026-10-09 was **12,652 lines for the day** against a largest other day of 28, in two buckets: **6,678 in the 15:00Z hour** and **5,954 in the 18:00Z hour**. Sampling 5,000 lines of the 17:00–19:30Z window (the instrument that can retrieve them) shows **2,485 of 2,492 `xrpc` lines are `GET /xrpc/space.roomy.space.getMetadata failed: Space not found`**, and the `xrpc` half names **2,480 distinct DIDs** — i.e. very nearly one request, and therefore one new file, per DID. `/data` is a persistent Railway volume and per-space DBs are deliberately excluded from Litestream restore (`docker-entrypoint.sh`), so the files are permanent.
  **It also costs the real spaces their cache.** `roomy_db_open_space_dbs` reads **100 on all eight space workers** right now — the `maxSpaceDbs` ceiling (`db/bounds.ts:16`) — with a 7 d maximum of 100 and a minimum of 0 (a boot). A ceiling this tight means each phantom file also runs `analyzeSpaceDb` and closes a *real* space DB on the way in (`db/worker.ts:480-499`).
- **Why now.** This is a storage and cache-cost defect that a public, unauthenticated query produces, it is measurable in milliseconds and bytes, and the burst that produced the most of it is seven days old and unexamined.
- **Scope sketch.** Two parts. (a) Establish existence before opening: read `comp_space` — or the global `entity_space` index the sibling handlers already use for exactly this question (`db/db.ts:159`) — and 404 without opening a DB. (b) Make the residue recoverable: delete the file a failed lookup just created, or add a boot sweep that removes space DBs with no `entity_space` rows. Do **not** change the `getMetadata` access rules — a non-member must still get metadata for the join screen (that is why the ban check comes first), only the order of the DB open and the existence read.
- **Acceptance.** A `getMetadata` for an unknown DID leaves no `${spaceDid}.sqlite` in `spacesDir`, and its latency stops including a schema exec (~88 ms in the reproduction above). After a deploy, `roomy_db_open_space_dbs` stops sitting at 100 on every worker.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no user-visible error, but an unauthenticated query writes permanent files to the production volume, and the same query evicts real spaces from an LRU that is already pinned at its ceiling.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `openSpaceDb` ordering, `spacesDir`, `roomy_db_open_space_dbs`, or the 2026-10-09 `Space not found` burst. The 10-17 record raised the burst as a *log-attribution* question; this is the mechanism, and the attribution it was missing is in this record's log findings.

### 2. The sidebar's read-position anchor is a `group by room` max over every message in every channel, on the hottest query in the app, and it is the largest `db-slow` shape

- **Evidence.** `ensureReadPositions` (`queries/readPositions.ts:117`) anchors a reader's missing rows with
  `select room as room_id, max(sort_idx) as newest from entities where room in (select value from json_each(?1)) group by room` (`:126-133`).
  It is called from `resolveSpaceUnreadCandidates` for **every channel and every engaged thread** of a sidebar load (`:371`, `:400`), which runs on `space.getMetadata` — **12,255 requests in 24 h**, the second-highest-volume route in the process.
  **In `db-slow` it is the largest single shape:** **98 rows in 24 h and 12,454 in 7 d**, against 1,186 for the by-id `selectMessages` and 813 for `delete from mentions where message_id`. Daily series `10-12=190, 10-13=748, 10-14=6649, 10-15=4122, 10-16=525, 10-17=122, 10-18=98` — it tracked the user_blocks burst and decayed with it, and what is left is the steady state. Percentiles over the sampled corpus: **p50 67 ms, p90 715 ms, p99 1,199 ms, max 1,581 ms** (24 h only: p50 95, p90 770, max 1,083).
  **The index is not the problem; the aggregate is.** The plan already uses `idx_entities_room_sort (room, sort_idx)` — but `max()` over `group by room` must walk **every entity of every room in the list** to find each maximum, per call. Rewriting it as one correlated seek per room (`select r.value, (select sort_idx from entities where room = r.value and sort_idx is not null order by sort_idx desc limit 1) from json_each(?1) r`) changes the plan to `SCAN r` + `CORRELATED SCALAR SUBQUERY` + `SEARCH … (room=? AND sort_idx>?)`, and measures **43.33 ms → 0.08 ms** at 400k entities / 120 rooms and **11.73 ms → 0.04 ms** at 122k / 60 (throwaway `bun:sqlite` benchmarks against the real `schema-space.sql`; identical rows after dropping the empty rooms, verified by comparing both result sets).
- **Why now.** It is the same defect class as `#480` and `#481` (an index-shaped answer to a scan) but with a different fix — the statement, not the schema — and it is on the path every sidebar load takes, so its tail lands on a shared `space-N` worker while a user is waiting for their sidebar.
- **Scope sketch.** Rewrite the anchor statement in place; keep the `json_each` parameter so one round-trip still answers every room, and keep the `newest is null → "0"` fallback. Note the identical statement also exists in the one-off `anchorPlaceholderReadPositions` (`db/readStatePositionsMigration.ts:107`); it runs only during the read-state migration, so it can either share the rewrite or be left alone — state which.
- **Acceptance.** `explain query plan` shows the correlated seek and no aggregate over `entities`; the benchmark's median falls below 1 ms on the 400k seed; the statement leaves `[db-slow]` (it is currently 98 rows/24 h at p50 95 ms, so its disappearance is observable within a day).
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no wrong answer is served, but p90 is 715 ms on the route every client calls first, and the fix is one statement.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `ensureReadPositions`, the read-position anchor, `readPositions.ts`, or `idx_entities_room_sort`. `#480` is the search backfill's sweep, `#481` is the participation gate, `#479` is a different `readPositions`-adjacent file (watermarks, not positions) — none of them is this statement.

### 3. `[db-slow]` cannot say which database was slow: 99.99% of its rows claim `targetDb: "events"` and `spaceDid` is absent

- **Evidence.** `WorkerLink.send` stores the request in `#pending` **before** the route is merged into the message: `this.#pending.set(id, { …, req })` (`db/asyncDatabase.ts:264-271`), then `this.#worker.postMessage({ ...req, ...route, id })` (`:272`). The route is posted to the worker and never written back onto `entry.req`, but the slow-request log reads it from there: `targetDb: entry.req.targetDb ?? "events"` and `spaceDid: entry.req.spaceDid` (`:209-210`).
  **Measured over the 20,973 sampled `db-slow` rows (7 d):** **20,972 read `targetDb: "events"` and exactly 1 reads `global`**; **320 carry `spaceDid`, and every one of them is a type that passes `spaceDid` in the request body itself** (`backfillEntitySpace` 197, `checkSpaceSchema` 82, `spaceMigrationBegin` 40, `spaceRebuildBegin` 1). On the deployed build `a691477a` alone the split is **2,670 rows, all `events`, 22 with `spaceDid`**. So every slow per-space query, every slow global write and every slow readstate transaction is filed as an event-log query.
  **What that costs, concretely:** the 16.5-minute stall on 2026-10-17T17:50:18Z reads as a `space-2` event-log `query` — and there is no event-log query anywhere near that statement; it is a per-space `selectMessages`. The record's own attribution work (this proposal's predecessor measurement, and the 10-17 record's proposal 2) had to be done by reading the SQL text because the fields lie.
- **Why now.** It is a one-line bug in the instrument that four consecutive records have leaned on, and it is the reason the biggest tail events in the sample cannot be localised without prose-parsing SQL.
- **Scope sketch.** Stamp the route onto the pending entry so the log reads what was actually sent — e.g. keep `req` as the un-merged body and record `route` alongside it, then log `entry.route?.targetDb ?? entry.req.targetDb ?? "events"` and `entry.route?.spaceDid ?? entry.req.spaceDid`. The worker already receives the merged object; only the bookkeeping is wrong.
- **Acceptance.** New `db-slow` rows carry the DB they targeted: a per-space slow query names its `spaceDid`, a readstate transaction says `readstate`, and `targetDb="events"` becomes the minority that genuinely is the event log. Asserted in a unit test on `WorkerLink`'s slow path if one can capture the log record, following the pattern `#479` uses for the watermark sweep.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3** — developer observability, no user-visible behaviour — but it is verified, reproducible from the log alone, and everything else in the pool-saturation story is currently un-attributable because of it.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `[db-slow]` fields, `WorkerLink.send`, or `targetDb`. TASK-164 is a task's *deploy* claim vs `/health`; this is the appserver's own log vs its own requests.

### 4. `search.messages` does up to one DB round-trip per hit, sequentially, inside the request

- **Evidence.** The handler over-fetches `limit × 10` (`handlers/space.roomy.search.messages.ts:53`, `:195`), hydrates per space in one batch, then walks the surviving hits **one at a time** (`:308`), and for each hit whose `replyTo` is set issues **two more sequential round-trips** — `openSpaceDbForEntity(message.replyTo)` then a single-id `selectMessages` (`:331-345`). With the client's `limit=20` that is up to 20 sequential hits plus 2 × (hits with replies), on top of the batched hydrate and the two per-space display-name queries.
  **Measured:** `space.roomy.search.messages` GET requests over 7 d: **39** (plus 35 `OPTIONS`), p50 **8.5 ms**, **p90 3.5 s**, **p99 4.85 s**; both 24 h and 7 d land on the same p90, and the histogram's last bucket (≤5 s) holds essentially all of the tail. This is the worst p90/p50 ratio of any route in the process (compare `roomy_db_wait_seconds` p99 ≤ 203 ms on the busiest worker).
- **Why now.** Search is the one route where a user waits, and a multi-second search reads as a broken feature. The mechanism is a loop over a request, which is exactly the shape `#481` and this record's proposal 2 are about — but here the fix is batching, not an index.
- **Scope sketch.** Batch the reply context: collect the distinct `replyTo` ids of the page, resolve their rooms from `entity_space` in one query, `roomAccessMany` them with the existing per-space memo, then one `selectMessages({ kind: "ids" })` per space for all of them — the same shape the handler already uses for `bySpace` hydration. Keep the "reply unavailable" fallback for ids that resolve to nothing.
- **Acceptance.** A `limit=20` search issues a constant number of statements independent of how many hits carry replies (the per-space hydrate plus one reply batch), and the endpoint's p90 drops out of the 2.5 s+ buckets. The observable check is the handler's statement count on a seeded space with replies, not a wall-clock number.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3** — the tail is the worst in the process but the volume is 39 requests/week, so no user is hitting it repeatedly; P2 would overstate the evidence.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `search.messages`, `OVERFETCH`, or reply denormalisation. `#475` is the mentions index, `#480` the backfill sweep, #476 the e2e coverage of search — none is the request path.
