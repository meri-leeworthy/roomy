### 1. The search-backfill sweep re-derives a space's whole timeline in a temp B-tree sort on every cycle, and one hit 11.9 s

- **Evidence.** `select e.id as id, e.room as room, cc.mime_type … from entities e left join comp_content cc … where e.stream_id = ? and e.room is not null and cc.entity is not null and (? is null or e.id > ?) order by e.id limit ?` (`search/backfill.ts:226-245`, `sweepOneSpace`; the predicate is unique to that file). In the `db-slow` sample over 24 h it appears **7 times with p50 1724 ms and max 11,869 ms**, and over 7 d **1,786 times with 11 rows over a second and 5 over five seconds** — the 7 d figure is dominated by the 2026-10-15 re-index burst (1,052 on 10-14, 683 on 10-15 against 8–23 on the days either side), which is the shape of a full sweep after the collection was re-created. The worst single row is `2026-10-16T16:50:17.951Z`, worker `space-7`, `selfMs` 11,869 of an 11,870 ms total — the time is the statement, not the queue.
  **The plan is a scan, and this run reproduced it.** With the planner's statistics present — the condition production is in, because `streams/reMaterialize.ts:285` runs `analyze()` per space on **every boot**, and the comment above it names this exact failure mode — `explain query plan` on the real `schema-space.sql` with 400,000 entities in one space is `SCAN e USING INDEX sqlite_autoindex_entities_1` then `USE TEMP B-TREE FOR ORDER BY`. Adding `create index if not exists idx_entities_stream_id_id on entities(stream_id, id)` turns it into `SEARCH e USING INDEX idx_entities_stream_id_id (stream_id=?)` and **drops the temp B-tree sort entirely**, because `id` is the table's rowid and the index therefore yields the required `order by e.id` for free.
  **The cursor does not bound the cost.** The space is re-picked once its `updated_at` is the oldest (`nextCursorSpace`, `backfill.ts:572-586`), so a large space is swept again and again as it ages; the `(? is null or e.id > ?)` form also defeats the index on `stream_id` alone, since a parameter that may be null cannot be turned into a range seek.
- **Why now.** This is the write path of the search index and it runs on the shared `space-N` workers that also serve user reads. It is the only one of the three large scan shapes in `[db-slow]` with no open PR: the `mentions` scan is `#475` and the room_activity rebuild is `#472`. Nine spaces have already taken the re-index burst, and Roomy Space alone is ~122k messages.
- **Scope sketch.** One additive index in `schema-space.sql`, following the file's existing `create index if not exists` convention (the DDL is already additive and idempotent; `spaceMigrations.test.ts` covers re-creation). Nothing else changes: `sweepOneSpace` keeps its shape, and the `order by e.id` is served by the index. Do **not** touch the round-robin or the cursor semantics.
- **Acceptance.** After the change, `explain query plan` for that statement shows a `SEARCH … USING INDEX idx_entities_stream_id_id` and no `USE TEMP B-TREE FOR ORDER BY`, and the statement's p50 in a `bun:sqlite` benchmark over a seeded 400k-entity space falls below the 1,724 ms measured here. The statement must then stop appearing in `[db-slow]`.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no wrong answer is served, but the tail is 11.9 s, it is measured, the fix is one line, and it is the last unbounded scan shape on the shared workers.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `search/backfill.ts`, `sweepOneSpace`, the search sweeper's round-robin, or `idx_entities_stream_id_id`. `#475` is the `mentions` table and `#472` is `room_activity`; both are different statements on different workers.
