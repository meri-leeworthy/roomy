## Proposals (ranked)

### 1. The bridge polls `admin.getSpaceMembership` ~30,800 times a day — 33.8% of every XRPC call the appserver serves — and 62% of those land in twelve one-second bursts an hour

- **Evidence.** `space.roomy.admin.getSpaceMembership` is the **busiest route on the appserver** by a wide margin. From Mimir over 24 h (`increase(roomy_xrpc_requests_total[24h])` by `endpoint`): **30,794**, against `space.getMetadata` 12,299, `room.getMessages` 9,249, `sendEvents` 7,143, `getActivityFeed` 5,941, `getSpaces` 4,972, `message.getMessage` 4,326, `getConnectionTicket` 4,282. Its share of the **91,324** XRPC calls in that window is **33.8%** (`sum(increase(...{endpoint="/xrpc/space.roomy.admin.getSpaceMembership"}[24h])) / sum(increase(...[24h]))` → 0.3381), and it has exactly one caller: the Discord bridge's capacity gate (`discord-bridge/src/roomy/membership-client.ts:65`).
  **The cadence is two populations stacked, and one of them is a thundering herd.** A 2 h raw corpus of the endpoint's own log lines (`{service_name="appserver"} |= "getSpaceMembership → 200"`, 2,868 lines covering 120 minutes) splits cleanly: **1,791 calls (62%) land in 24 minutes** — mean **74.6**, every burst exactly on the minute boundary (second offset 0), spaced **precisely 5.0 minutes apart** (`05:26, 05:31, 05:36, …`) — and the remaining **1,077 (38%) spread over 72 minutes at ~15/min**. The 15/min is `isEnabled` being consulted by ingestion, room sync, profile sync and backfill (`message-ingestion.ts:206,480,625`, `room-sync.ts:41,186,569`, `profile-sync.ts:54,130`, `backfill.ts:296,434,521,664,867,1270,1526`); the 74.6 is one sweep, because 75 tuples each force a check.
  The sweep is `setInterval(…, 5 * 60 * 1000)` → `checkAllBridges` (`discord-bridge/src/index.ts:244-250`, `:151-156`), which loops `repo.listAllBridgeConfigs()` and calls `capacity.check(guildId, spaceDid, { force: true })` for every tuple — `force` bypasses the cache (`capacity.ts:235`) and each miss reaches the appserver (`:255-259`). Twelve sweeps an hour × 75 = **900 calls/hour of the ~1,300–1,440 the endpoint actually serves**, which is the arithmetic the corpus confirms.
  **Two costs, both real.** (a) The appserver's `#465` commit message (2026-10-15) records that a read-path fan-out saturated the read-state worker; this is the same shape on a fixed 75-per-tick timer rather than a per-read one. (b) `#498` (open, unreviewed) measured the same endpoint at "23,300 calls/24 h" and removed its per-request `select count(*) from edges`; it is **30,854 now**, so whatever that PR fixed, the rate is untouched and has grown with the tuple count. `db-slow` shows no membership statement in its top 12 shapes over 24 h, so the *per-call* cost is small; the *rate* is the problem.
- **Why now.** It is a third of the production traffic for a gate whose answer changes when a guild's member count crosses a threshold, not every five minutes. Every sweep is also a forced cache miss for tuples whose TTL (`CAPACITY_TTL_MS = 300_000`, `capacity.ts:47`) had not expired; the sweep exists to defeat the very cache it set.
- **Scope sketch.** In the bridge, not the appserver, and small: make the sweep *cooperative* rather than instantaneous — iterate the tuple list with a per-tuple cache-respecting `check` (no `force`) and a bounded concurrency of ~4, so 75 tuples spread across the tick instead of landing in one second; and raise the sweep interval while keeping the cache TTL as the freshness bound, since `force: true` is what makes the interval meaningless. A member-add/remove gateway event already triggers a check, so the periodic sweep is only a backstop for missed events. If the decision is instead "the TTL is the only cadence we need", then delete the sweep and record why. Either way, **read `#498` first**: it changes the same endpoint's response and is unreviewed, and this proposal's acceptance can be checked without conflicting with it.
- **Acceptance.** `increase(roomy_xrpc_requests_total{endpoint="/xrpc/space.roomy.admin.getSpaceMembership"}[1h])` falls from ~1,440 to the same order as the other bridge-consulted routes (a few hundred), with no sweep second carrying more than a handful of calls; a capacity state change still disables sync within one TTL; `bun test --cwd packages/discord-bridge` green.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no data is lost and no user sees an error, but it is a third of production request volume spent on a cache-defeating poll, and it is the cheapest large ratio available.
- **Not already tracked because.** No open task, plan or PR names the sweep cadence or `checkAllBridges`. `#498` is the *response shape and per-call cost* of the same endpoint; its own body claims the call volume as evidence and leaves it in place.

### 2. A Discord edit is relayed once per `MESSAGE_UPDATE` event, and the bridge keeps no record of which edits it already sent — 100 of 433 edits in 7 days were applied twice, 198 extra events

- **Evidence.** `handleMessageEdit` (`discord-bridge/src/services/message-edit-delete.ts:24`) builds a **fresh `editMessage` event with a new ULID on every invocation** (`const eventUlid = newUlid();` at `:105`) and sends it via `sendEventOrQueue` (`:155-161`). There is no watermark anywhere on the path: no stored "last edit sent for this Discord message", no compare against `message.editedTimestamp` beyond the presence check at `:34` (`if (!message.editedTimestamp) return;`), and `sendEventOrQueue` (`services/send-retry.ts:62-93`) dedupes nothing — it sends or queues.
  **Measured** over a 7 d raw corpus of `Synced edit` lines (631 lines): **433 distinct `(discordId, roomyMessageId)` pairs**, of which **100 pairs were applied more than once** — **198 extra edit events**. **89 of those repeats are within 2 seconds of the previous one**, which is a duplicate delivery of the same edit rather than a user editing twice. The worst case is 21 applications of one message (`1562236990978527313 → 01M5FYN…`) across 894 seconds. Only **6** of the 89 same-second pairs fall within 30 s of a bridge WebSocket close, so reconnect replay is not the explanation — the events arrive as distinct gateway deliveries.
  The write path is not free: each edit is an ATProto record, a materialised row, an unread/`roomMeta` diff fan-out, an embed sweep if the body carries a URL, and a search-index row. The bridge's own header (`message-edit-delete.ts:40-45`) already names the webhook/REST race it guards for *creates* but not for edits.
- **Why now.** It is pure amplification of the most expensive write the system has, on a path with no user-visible symptom — so nothing else will ever report it. The measured rate (23% of edits duplicated) is high enough that the missing guard is a likely contributor to any write-cost work, and the fix is one persisted field.
- **Scope sketch.** Persist `(spaceDid, discordId) → lastAppliedEditTimestamp` in the bridge's SQLite next to the existing roomy-id mapping, and drop an invocation whose `editedTimestamp` is `<=` the stored value. That is the same shape as the roomy-id mapping the handler already reads (`repo.getRoomyId`) and needs no schema change beyond one column or one key namespace. Do **not** try to make the event id deterministic — the bridge's retry queue resends the identical payload on purpose, and two genuinely different edits must still produce two events.
- **Acceptance.** Re-running the 7 d measurement shows no `(discordId, roomyMessageId)` pair applied twice inside a second, and two edits made deliberately in sequence still both land; a unit test drives `handleMessageEdit` twice with the same `editedTimestamp` and asserts one `sendEvents` call, and once with a newer timestamp and asserts two.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — a 23% duplicate rate on the highest-cost write path, with no data loss either way (the last edit wins), so it is amplification, not corruption.
- **Not already tracked because.** `#473` (open) is the *send queue's* duplicate re-offer landings for creates and is fixed there; `TASK-22` is attachment link previews; nothing in the queue or any PR names edit idempotency.

### 3. The bridge's sync WebSocket dies 365 times a day and reconnects in a median of 3.5 s — but there is no record on either side of what it missed while it was down

- **Evidence.** Over 24 h the bridge logged **365 `Connection closed … code=1006`** lines (7 d: 1,710; 30 d: 4,790), against **2** clean `1000` closes. The closes are spread over **15 of the 15 bridged spaces** and are not a server-wide event: per space the median gap between one close and the next is **~28 minutes**, and only 11 of 350 gaps are under 60 s.
  Each close is followed by a reconnect: **662 `reconnecting → open` transitions in 48 h**, median lag **3.5 s**, p90 8.6 s, max 286 s — **5,904 s of cumulative downtime, ~49 minutes a day** across all spaces. Connection lifetimes (open → next `reconnecting`) over 48 h, n=648: median **2,130 s**, p10 240 s, p90 8,312 s, with 18 under a minute and 215 over an hour. So this is not a tight crash loop; it is a steady ~2-minute-per-hour-per-space drop.
  **The gap is the record, not the drop.** On reconnect the gateway resubscribes from the persisted cursor (`live-gateway.ts:189-207`, `#repo.getSpaceCursor(spaceDid) ?? -1`) and the appserver backfills from `cursor`. But **nothing on either side states what was recovered**: the bridge logs `Subscribed to events from …` **4 times in 7 d** (it is logged once at first subscribe, not on reconnect), there is no `roomy_*` metric for sync connections (`{__name__=~"roomy_.*"}` has no sync/ws series at all), and the appserver's per-connection close path (`xrpc/router.ts:425-428`) only calls `abort.abort()` and the handler's `onClose` — no log line, no counter. A bridge that silently misses a window therefore looks identical to one that caught up.
  The `heartbeat: {}` option is enabled (`live-gateway.ts:167`) and Bun does expose `.ping` (`bun 1.3.14`, `typeof WebSocket.prototype.ping === "function"`), so the heartbeat path is live; the closes are not heartbeat timeouts either — `code=4000` (the heartbeat's own close code, `sdk/src/sync/connection.ts:467`) appears **6 times in 7 d**.
- **Why now.** ~49 minutes a day of dropped socket per space set is the exact precondition for the class of bug the bridge exists to prevent — a Discord message that never reaches Roomy — and the reason we cannot say whether it has happened is that recovery is unobservable. This is also the ground truth behind `#503`'s "count drops durably" framing: the drops it counts are *ingest* drops, not *transport* gaps.
- **Scope sketch.** Two counters and one line, not a transport change. In the bridge, log (at `info`, with fields) the cursor resumed from and the number of backfilled events, once per reconnect, not once per subscribe. In the appserver, count `sync.subscribe` upgrades and closes by close code on the existing metrics registry so `rate(roomy_sync_closes_total[1h])` exists. Leave the reconnect policy alone — the measured backoff is working, and the 1006s are most likely the server or a proxy dropping idle sockets, which the heartbeat already probes every 30 s.
- **Acceptance.** `roomy_sync_closes_total{code="1006"}` exists and matches the bridge's own close count within a few percent; a bridge reconnect emits one log line naming the resume cursor and the backfilled event count; a deliberately killed socket produces both.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3** — no observed message loss, and the reconnect is fast; but the measurement that would *prove* no loss does not exist today, and it is two counters.
- **Not already tracked because.** `TASK-356` is the runaway `bun test` taking a host out of monitorability (a different failure); `TASK-146(b)` is the SDK's silent-exit reproduction (a no-timer, no-log, no-exit state, which this data does **not** show — reconnects settle in 3.5 s). Nothing names transport-gap observability.

### 4. Editing a space role can fail with no visible error — the catch writes to the console and the modal just stays open

- **Evidence.** The permissions page swallows every failure. `[space]/settings/permissions/+page.svelte:91-99` (`onSaveEdit`) catches and does `console.error("Failed to update role", e)` — no toast, no inline error, and `editOpen` stays true, so the modal simply does not close. The sibling `onDelete` (`:101-119`) has the same shape, and `onCreate` (`:79-88`) has a bare `finally` with no catch at all, so a rejected `createRole` becomes an unhandled rejection the user never sees.
  **This is the `#bugs` report that was never answered.** Zack Brown, 2026-09-15T16:07Z, desktop app: *"Editing channel permissions and clicking Save does not work - kind of. You need to click Save like 4 or 5 times for it to actually save"* — the exact symptom of a handler that fails silently and needs a retry; six weeks on, the page still has no error surface. Contrast `[space]/settings/+page.svelte:152-181`, whose `save()` does `saveError = e instanceof Error ? e.message : "Failed to save changes"` and renders it (`:338`) — one settings surface explains itself and the other does not, which is a second convention beside the first.
- **Why now.** It is a real user report with a reproduced mechanism, still present on the audited ref, in the admin path of every space; and it costs one toast plus one inline error to close. The retry-4-or-5-times wording also points at a second question the fix should not silently answer: the handler never disables Save while in flight, so a slow `updateRole` plus an anxious second click can produce two events.
- **Scope sketch.** Give the permissions page the same error surface the space settings page already uses: a `roleError` state rendered beside the form (or a `toast.error` in the catch), and an in-flight flag that disables Save while `updateRole` is running. Apply the same treatment to `onCreate` and `onDelete` in the same file — they are three copies of one missing pattern. Do not change the mutation or the XRPC; the failure is display, and the underlying `updateRole` error message is already user-legible.
- **Acceptance.** A `updateRole` rejection renders a visible error on the permissions page (unit test on the component with a rejected mock, or one manual break: point `updateRole` at a non-existent role and confirm the page says so); Save is disabled while the call is in flight; the settings page's existing error behaviour is unchanged.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3** — no data loss and a retry eventually works, which is why it has survived six weeks; but "the save button does nothing" is the worst kind of admin-bug report and the fix is one file.
- **Not already tracked because.** `TASK-16` is the OAuth "token was not issued to this client" error and `TASK-19` is post-login auth; nothing in the queue names role editing, silent catches, or an admin-settings error surface.

## Limits of the evidence

- **Proposal 1's per-call cost is not measured.** I know the *call rate* (30,794/24 h; 75 per sweep second; 15 per off-sweep minute) and that the endpoint no longer appears in `db-slow`'s top shapes, which is consistent with `#498` having removed its per-request edge count. I did **not** measure CPU or latency per call, and `#498` is an open PR describing an intent, not a deployed fact. If `#498` merges and the rate is unchanged, this proposal's premise still holds; if the per-call cost turns out to be trivial, the value falls to "a third of the request count is a cache-defeating poll".
- **Proposal 2's duplicate rate is a log census, not a repro.** It counts `Synced edit` lines that reached `landed: true`, keyed by `(discordId, roomyMessageId)`. I did not reproduce a duplicate by driving two gateway events through the handler, and I did not confirm from Discord's side that a single user edit produces two `MESSAGE_UPDATE` deliveries — the 2-second spacing and the poor correlation with reconnects (6 of 89) point at duplicate delivery, but a double-tap at the *edit* level by a user would look similar. The 21-times case over 894 seconds is almost certainly a long edit session, not a bug.
- **Proposal 3's 1006 closes have no identified cause.** The closes are spread across all 15 spaces, the reconnect backoff is healthy, the heartbeat is enabled and Bun exposes `.ping`, and `code=4000` (the heartbeat's own timeout close) is rare — so it is *not* the heartbeat declaring the socket dead. What drops the socket (Bun's `idleTimeout: 255`, a Railway proxy, or the appserver's own close path) is not established, and the proposal deliberately does not guess: it asks for the counters that would answer it.
- **Proposal 4's mechanism is a source read plus an unconfirmed user report.** I read the three handlers and confirmed none renders an error. I did not reproduce the "click Save 4 or 5 times" symptom, and the reporter was on the desktop app, which I did not run — a Tauri-only failure (e.g. a store write that rejects) would produce the same symptom from a different cause.
