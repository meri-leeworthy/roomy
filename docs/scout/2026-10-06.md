### 1. 47,625 read-state watermarks were left unresolved by the v3 re-key, and nothing measures or repairs them

- **Evidence.** The migration logs one line per space (`db/spaceMigrations.ts:297-301`). Aggregated over the 4,411 of them in Loki: **1,036,116 ordering events replayed, 2,638 watermarks re-anchored, 47,625 left unresolved** — and unresolved is counted only for values that are neither `""` nor `"0"` (`:161`), i.e. every one of those 47,625 is a user's real last-read position. **142 spaces are affected, and in them 47,625 of 49,159 watermarks (96.9%) did not resolve**; across all 4,411 spaces the unresolved share is 94.7% of 50,263. The distribution is concentrated: `did:plc:4moccs43r5v2xzkynae3xk2u` (Muni Town) **25,255 unresolved of 25,815**, `did:plc:gnwy2zbm3hu4gfdawzxmpb2s` (Roomy Space) **21,170 of 21,368** — the two together are **97.5% of the total** — then `ivppxz…` 145, `quqz6…` 131, `b6csk…` 124, and 134 of the 142 have fewer than 60. For comparison, Meri's Forest re-anchored 325 against 13 unresolved.
  The consequence is a source reading of the same table that consumes it: `seen_up_to` **is** the sort key every unread computation compares against — `updateSeen`'s `count(*) … where sort_idx > ?` (`handlers/space.roomy.room.updateSeen.ts:89`), `decrementUnreadForRemovedMessages`'s `s > row.seen_up_to` (`materialization/roomDerivedState.ts:109`), and `decodeSeenUpTo`, which maps an undecodable value to "no real watermark" (`queries/readPositions.ts:28-40`). A watermark that names no entity is the last of those: the row still carries its stored `unread_count`, but the marker is gone and the next write to that room re-derives from an absent key.
  The resolver is deliberate and well-commented — it follows each watermark through `sort_idx_prev` rather than guessing, and counts what it cannot follow (`:117-135`) — and `sort_idx_prev` is dropped once it has run (`:295`), so the pre-clear keys are not available for a retry.
- **Why now.** This is the largest residue of the deploy that cost a day, it is invisible outside one log line, and the two spaces that hold 97% of it are the two the loop itself works in — Muni Town is where every task and every agent report lives. A user whose watermark did not resolve has a room whose unread state is simply wrong.
- **Scope sketch.** Two separable pieces. (a) Make the count observable: a `roomy_readstate_watermarks_unresolved` gauge (or one line per space at `warn` rather than a field inside an `info` summary) so this can never again be a number only one person reads in a log grep. (b) A repair pass: for a watermark that resolved to nothing, the room's own newest page is the honest source — the user's position can be re-anchored to the greatest `coalesce(sort_idx, id)` the read path would have shown them, or the row can be reset to "everything read" and the unread count recomputed, which is what `updateSeen` does when it is handed no watermark at all. Which of those is right is a product call and should be made in the brief, not in the worker.
- **Acceptance.** The gauge exists and reads 47,625 (or the true current figure) before the repair; after it, Muni Town and Roomy Space report 0 unresolved and a spot-checked user's room unread count matches what the room's timeline shows.
- **Suggested priority:** **P1** — 47,625 wrong read positions across the two most-used spaces, with no monitor.
- **Not already tracked because.** TASK-289 is the incident (loop, write-gate, rollback predicate) and its verdicts are in; this is what the successful migration left behind. K's 10:28Z report states it as a measurement (its residual 2) and explicitly did not turn it into a task.

### 2. `[db-slow]` logs 25,399 lines a day at a 50 ms threshold, and two-thirds of them are one worker

- **Evidence.** `#366` (`fdc88fa8`, deployed on client build `b9a83213`) added `log.warn("[db-slow] request", …)` (`db/asyncDatabase.ts:205-215`) gated by the worker's own service time at `SLOW_REQUEST_MS = 50` (`db/worker.ts:120,763`). Live counts on the deployed build: **25,399 lines in 24 h, 11,637 in 6 h, 5,596 in 2 h** — one line every 3.4 s, sustained. The hourly series runs 815–5,863 with no quiet hour.
  From a 3,000-line sample of one 2 h window: **`space-1` 1,896 (63%)**, then `space-3` 237, `space-2` 206, `global` 136, `space-6` 117; `selfMs` median 92.5 ms, p95 **4,513 ms**, max 16,945 ms.   The threshold is below the healthy cost of the busiest read: `/metrics` on the same build gives `GET space.roomy.space.getMetadata` a **30 s p95** at n=822 and a 3.1 s mean, and `getMetadata`'s own bucket count shows only 341 of 930 requests under 10 ms.
- **Why now.** The instrument exists to attribute slow DB requests to a worker — which it does, and which is how TASK-291's subject (per-space tail latency) is measurable at all — but at this rate it is a firehose in the same subsystem whose log volume TASK-143 and TASK-142 exist to reduce, and a signal that fires 25,399 times a day cannot be alerted on. This is also independent corroboration of the skew CF6 measures: `space-1` carries most of the tail as well as most of the volume.
- **Scope sketch.** Do not remove the instrument. (a) Raise the threshold, or make it a ratio of the request's route p95 rather than an absolute 50 ms — "slow" only means something relative to what is normal for that query. (b) Sample: log the first N per worker per window plus the true tail, and keep the histogram (`roomy_db_wait_seconds`, already labelled by worker and type) as the unsampled record, which is what an alert should read anyway.
- **Acceptance.** On a build carrying the change, `count_over_time({…} |= "db-slow" [1h])` falls by ≥90% while a request with `selfMs` ≥ 1,000 still produces a line; `roomy_db_wait_seconds` is unchanged in shape.

### 4. A dead session now leaves the client in a "session expired" loop that stops reloading and never logs out — 111 telemetry lines in one two-hour burst

- **Evidence.** Production `roomy-app-lite` telemetry, one Chrome session, 2026-10-05T18:45:49Z → 20:28:29Z: **111 lines**, of which `[error-recovery] Recoverable ATProto error detected — reloading page in 600ms. Error: session expired` × 8, `…auto-reload limit is reached — refusing to reload automatically to avoid a loop` × 3, and the rest the same failure reported by every caller that touches the session in turn — `Failed to fetch profile`, `Failed to record scope grant`, `[push] could not fetch VAPID public key`, `[push:login] background re-subscribe failed`, and a bare `console.error: session expired`. The same shape recurs at 19:57Z and 20:28Z (and once on 10-02T16:25Z), so it is repeatable rather than a one-off.
  The mechanism is in `packages/app-lite/src/lib/error-recovery.ts`: `MAX_RELOADS = 3` within a 60 s window (`:40,42`, enforced at `:165`), after which the page stops reloading — correctly, to avoid a loop — and the console line is the only record. There is no path from here to `logout()`. On the server side the same window is **completely clean**: 0 appserver `error` lines between 18:30Z and 20:45Z, so nothing here is an outage.
- **Why now.** It is a dead end the user cannot get out of without clearing site data, and it has a maintainer note behind it: Zicklag in Muni Town `#roomy`, 2026-09-29T23:39Z — *"you may have to logout manually because the 'session expired' error doesn't automatically log you out. We probably need to fix that."* That was during the session migration; this is the first measured instance since, five days later, and it is the same signature.
- **Scope sketch.** When the reload budget is exhausted for a *session* failure specifically (as distinct from a stale-chunk failure, which is transient by construction), the client should stop retrying and take the user to the login path — the `auth` module already owns that transition. Keep the budget; change what happens when it is spent.
- **Acceptance.** With the token store forced invalid, the app reaches a login prompt within the reload budget instead of settling into a console-error loop; a unit test on the exhausted-budget branch asserting the recovery action is a logout rather than a no-op.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no data loss and no server-side cause, but an unrecoverable UI state for a user whose session simply expired.
- **Not already tracked because.** TASK-16 (`Token was not issued to this client`) and TASK-19 (intermittent post-login auth failures) are both about auth *failing on the way in*; this is the client's behaviour *after* the session dies, and nothing in the queue mentions `error-recovery` or the reload budget.

### 5. Bridged replies to a message containing a link re-embed the quoted link, and the 50-character quote can cut a URL in half

- **Evidence.** Reported in Roomy Space `#bugs`, 2026-10-06T00:04:06Z, by Lino Le Van — *"bridged replies to stuff that has links is pretty janky"*, *"probably shouldn't try to re-embed"*, *(on the discord side)* — and acknowledged by Meri at 02:44Z: *"Oh yeah, that's bad!"*. Mechanism, read on `1892e871`, `packages/discord-bridge/src/services/roomy-event-router.ts`:
  - `#buildReplyPrefix` (`:959-993`) renders a reply as grey text `-# ↪ <discord link> <snippet>`, where the snippet is the **parent's own Discord content**, sliced to `QUOTE_MAX_LENGTH = 50` with `ownText.slice(0, 50)` and then `...`.
  - `stripFauxPrefixLines` (`:145-147`) exists precisely so the parent's *own* text is quoted — but the parent's own text is exactly where a user's link lives. Discord unfurls any URL in a message body, so quoting a link re-creates the embed the parent already carries, which is the complaint.
  - The slice is character-based, so a URL in the quoted region is truncated mid-string: Discord then renders a broken link rather than either a card or plain text.
  - The bridge goes to some trouble to avoid the mirror of this on the way in — `decodeBody`'s comment (`:187-192`) unwraps markdown autolinks specifically *"so Discord embeds the link like a normal one"* — which is consistent with the outbound case being unintended.
- **Why now.** It is user-reported today, maintainer-acknowledged the same day, on the bridge's most common interactive shape (a reply), and it is one of the few pieces of this report a reader can check by looking at Discord.
- **Scope sketch.** In `#buildReplyPrefix` only: neutralise URLs inside the quoted snippet (wrap in `<>`, which Discord treats as embed suppression — the same trick `decodeBody` documents in the other direction), and truncate on a boundary that cannot split a URL. Do not change the prefix's shape, its link, or `#buildForwardContent`'s behaviour without a second report.
- **Acceptance.** A bridge reply to a parent whose own text is a bare URL renders in Discord with one embed (the parent's) and a grey quote line whose text contains no unfurled second card; a unit test on `#buildReplyPrefix` asserting the snippet it returns carries no bare URL when the parent's own text is a URL.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — cosmetic on the Discord side, but it is the reported symptom and the fix is local.
- **Not already tracked because.** TASK-22 is about *embedding* links that Discord sends *into* Roomy (the opposite direction) and is untouched by this; nothing in the queue mentions faux replies.

### 6. The client's persisted cache drops ~51,000 entries a day at a 200-entry cap, and the diagnostic cannot say which queries they were

- **Evidence.** Production `roomy-app-lite`: `cache: dropped N entries over the persistence budget` **249 lines in 24 h** (330 in 72 h), summing **51,371 entries dropped in 24 h**, with individual saves dropping as many as **523** entries. The rule is `MAX_PERSISTED_ENTRIES = 200` (`packages/app-lite/src/lib/client.ts:122`, applied at `:152`), enforced newest-first by `boundEntries` (`packages/sdk/src/cache/bound.ts:32-46`), which emits the diagnostic and nothing else — no nsid, no key.
  Context for the cap's fit: the appserver serves **4,411 spaces** and the two spaces this loop uses report hundreds of rooms between them; the client also persists per-room message pages. Against that, a 200-entry budget means the oldest retained entry is whatever survived the last save, and the daily drop count says the working set is far above it.
- **Why now.** The 24-hour `maxAge` is exactly what Meri asked to remove this morning (TASK-290: *"we would prefer to try having it not expire, and show the stale result while revalidating even if it's very stale"*). The count budget is the **other** bound on the same cache and is much tighter than the age one being removed, so changing the age alone leaves the restore path bounded by 200 entries and the same "why is my history cold" experience. There is also a diagnostic gap: `boundEntries` reports how many were dropped and never which, so the budget cannot be tuned from production evidence.
- **Scope sketch.** Decide the budget against the measured working set (or make it per-query-class rather than global), and extend the drop diagnostic to name the oldest dropped key(s) so the next run can size it. This is a measurement-plus-constant change, not a redesign; the persistence seam itself (`createCachePersistence`, `withFallback`) is fine.
- **Acceptance.** After a day on a build carrying the change, `count_over_time(… |= "persistence budget" [24h])` is materially below 249 and the sum of dropped entries is below a few hundred; and the drop line names at least one dropped key.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3** — no correctness impact (a dropped entry is a cold query, not a wrong one), but it bounds the fix Meri has already asked for and is currently unmeasurable.
- **Not already tracked because.** TASK-290 (dispatched today) covers the age and the stale-while-revalidate UI; `#374` covers the empty-versus-corrupt diagnostic in `readSnapshot`. Neither touches the count budget.
