## Proposals (ranked)

### 1. The multi-select delete and move paths send one unchunked `sendEvents` batch, and every selection over 50 messages is rejected whole

- **Evidence.** `deleteMessages` builds **one event per selected message** and hands the whole array to `sendEvents` in a single call (`app-lite/src/lib/mutations/message.ts:238-251`); `moveMessages` does the same (`:295-310`). Nothing chunks them. The endpoint caps the batch at 50 — `events: type.object.array().moreThanLength(0).atMostLength(50)` (`sdk/src/schemas/procedures/sendEvents.ts:15`) — and `sendEvents` passes the array through unmodified (`app-lite/src/lib/mutations/send-events.ts:7-14`). The sibling paths *do* chunk: `forwardMessages` (`message.ts:340-341`) and `createThread` (`thread.ts:49-50`) both loop `i += MAX_EVENTS_PER_SEND`, and that constant is declared for exactly this reason (`forward.ts:29-30`, with a unit test pinning it to 50 at `forward.test.ts:178`). Selection has no cap: `toggleMessageSelection` appends unconditionally (`components/chat/messaging-state.svelte.ts:177-193`).
  **Measured, this run:** the client logged `XRPC space.roomy.space.sendEvents failed (400): … events must be at most length 50 (was 71)` at **2026-10-19T04:20:22Z** — a 71-message selection refused in full. The appserver's paired `xrpc` half carries `POST /xrpc/space.roomy.space.sendEvents → 400`.
- **Why now.** The failure is total and silent: `DeleteMessageDialog.confirm` awaits `onConfirm()` inside `try/finally` **with no `catch`** (`design/src/components/content/thread/message/DeleteMessageDialog.svelte:42-51`) and `confirmDelete` has no guard of its own (`routes/[space]/[room]/+page.svelte:106-125`), so a rejected batch closes the dialog with no toast and deletes nothing — the user sees the same messages still there after confirming. The 400 surfaces only as an unhandled client exception. And it is the *newest* code path in the app: multi-select delete/move shipped 2026-09-16, and this is the first recorded instance of it failing.
- **Scope sketch.** Chunk in the two functions the way their siblings already do — reuse `MAX_EVENTS_PER_SEND` rather than re-deriving 50 — and decide the partial-failure contract explicitly, since a mid-selection failure leaves some messages deleted and some not; the dialog must keep itself open (or report) when a chunk fails instead of closing silently. No server change: the 50-event cap is the endpoint's contract and the forward path already respects it.
- **Acceptance.** A selection of 71 messages issues two `sendEvents` calls (50 + 21) and deletes all 71; a selection whose second chunk fails reports it and does not close as if it had succeeded. The observable check is the request log on a seeded room, plus a unit test over the chunk boundaries that fails before the change.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no data corruption and the messages are recoverable (they were never deleted), but a user who selects more than 50 messages loses the whole operation with no message saying why, on a feature that shipped a month ago.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `deleteMessages`, `moveMessages`, the selection size, or `MAX_EVENTS_PER_SEND`. `#457` is the message DTO's attachment list, `#469` is the multi-select toolbar's position — neither touches the batch.

### 2. `getActivityFeed` walks every joined space one at a time, three separate times, inside one request

- **Evidence.** `selectActivityFeed` has **three serial per-space loops in one request**, none of them `Promise.all`-ed: the page fetch (`queries/activityFeed.ts:165`, `for (const spaceDid of spaceDids) … await`), the full-message hydration (`:227`, `for (const [spaceDid, rows] of bySpace)`), and the per-space media/link/reaction batch (`:294`, `for (const [spaceDid, lastMsgIds] of lastMsgIdsBySpace)` — two awaited statements each). The sibling query does it the other way: `selectJoinedSpaces` fans out with `Promise.all` (`queries/joinedSpaces.ts:79`). Each iteration opens a per-space DB (`openSpaceDb`, `db/db.ts`) on a shared pool worker, so the request's latency is the **sum** over spaces rather than the max.
  **Measured:** `space.roomy.space.getActivityFeed` carries **p50 21.7 ms, p90 132 ms, p99 985 ms** over 24 h and **1,423 requests/24 h** (`roomy_xrpc_request_duration_seconds_bucket`, endpoint `/xrpc/…`). In the 24 h slow-request census it accounts for **89 of 318** responses over 500 ms, **more than any other route**, with a max of **18,236 ms** at 2026-10-18T18:50:18Z. The route has no `withSpan` anywhere — it is one of the two slowest routes in the process and **completely invisible to tracing** (Tempo's 100-trace sample over 7 d contains exactly two root names, `sendEvents` and `getMessages`).
- **Why now.** The feed is mounted on `/` for every signed-in reader (`routes/+page.svelte:68`) and it is the only route in the top four whose cost scales with how many spaces the reader belongs to. It is also the one slow path with no plan item and no open PR: `#480` is the search backfill, `#481` the participation gate, `#472` the thread-activity projection, `#488` the read-position anchor.
- **Scope sketch.** Convert the three loops to `Promise.all` over the distinct space DIDs — the loops are independent reads whose results are merged into shared maps afterwards, so the merge stays as-is and only the awaits change. The per-space `openSpaceDb` calls already return worker handles, so concurrency is bounded by the pool (8 space workers), not by the number of spaces. Add a `withSpan` around the handler so the next measurement can attribute the tail; the three phases are worth their own spans given the census above.
- **Acceptance.** A feed request issuing `N` space reads issues them concurrently: the handler's wall time on a seeded multi-space account falls to roughly the slowest space rather than the sum, and Tempo shows the route at all. The observable check is the span breakdown for one request, not a wall-clock number from this host.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — p90 132 ms is acceptable but p99 near a second, on the home route of every signed-in user, with a one-line fix per loop.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `selectActivityFeed`, `activityFeed.ts`, the feed's fan-out or its missing tracing. `TASK-143` is about agent-chatter write cost; `TASK-357` is a design-package move.

### 3. `sendEvents` waits for invalidation fan-out after the write commits, and that tail is the whole of its p99

- **Evidence.** The handler writes and materialises, then awaits the invalidation router inline: `await withSpan("sendEvents.write", …)` → `await withSpan("sendEvents.invalidation", …) → this.#invalidationRouter.onEventsApplied(...)` (`handlers/space.roomy.space.sendEvents.ts:204-217`; `streams/StreamManager.ts:261-272`). `handleCreateMessage` inside it does per-reader work **serially**: one `getRoomReadPositionWatermarks`, one `deriveUnreadCounts`, then a `for (const user of newlyUnread)` loop that awaits a **full `spaceHasUnreads` probe per newly-unread reader** (`invalidation/inferSignals.ts:447-451`, probing `queries/readPositions.ts:560`).
  **Measured, from a trace of the exact request the log shows as 7,063 ms:** `space.roomy.space.sendEvents` **7,061 ms** total, of which `sendEvents.write` **7,053 ms**, of which `sendEvents.invalidation` **7,013 ms**, of which **`router.inferSignals` 7,005 ms**; every other phase is sub-10 ms (`sendEvents.access` 6 ms, `sendEvents.authorize` 1 ms, `router.fetchMessageSnapshots` 0.9 ms). Two smaller traces repeat the shape: **2,200 ms** total with `inferSignals` 2,168 ms, and **701 ms** total with `inferSignals` 99 µs but `write.streamManager` 699 ms. Over 7 d the route's p50 is **31 ms** and its max **2,143 ms**, so this is a tail, not the norm — and `sendEvents` is the app's only write path.
- **Why now.** The write is already durable when this runs: `stream_events` and `stream_manager`'s transaction complete before `#runSerialized` starts the invalidation phase, so the client is waiting on fan-out it cannot act on before its own frame arrives. This is the same class the 10-18 record raised for the read paths, on the write path, and it is now the *largest* measured tail in the process.
- **Scope sketch.** Two changes, separable. (a) Bound the per-reader probe: `spaceHasUnreads` is called once per newly-unread user with a shared-memo optional argument that this call site leaves `undefined` — pass the request's memo (or batch the probes) so N readers do not mean N full candidate resolutions. (b) Decide whether the invalidation phase belongs in the request at all, or behind the response (the diff frames it produces are already pushed over the socket, and the handler's own reply is void). (a) is a clear win and independent; (b) is the one that needs a decision, so state which is taken.
- **Acceptance.** A single-message send on a room with many readers stays within a small multiple of the write's own time: the `router.inferSignals` span stops dominating `sendEvents.invalidation`, and `sendEvents`' p99 falls out of the seconds bucket. Measured off the span breakdown, before and after.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3** — a p50 of 31 ms means the common case is fine and this is a multi-reader tail; but every message in the product goes through it, and the trace names the exact function.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `sendEvents.invalidation`, `inferSignals`, `handleCreateMessage`'s reader loop or `spaceHasUnreads`. `TASK-143` is the *cost* of chatter (writes, sweeps, index rows), not the latency of one write's fan-out.

### 4. The query cache fills to one entry per caller per space and then evicts on every write; `getActivityFeed` and `roomThread.getMetadata` almost never hit

- **Evidence.** The cache is one process-wide LRU keyed by `(nsid, params, userDid)` with `maxEntries` **4096** (`cache/queryCache.ts:86`, `cache/index.ts:72`) and a 60 s TTL. Nothing partitions it by caller: every reader's `getMetadata` and `getActivityFeed` page is its own entry.
  **Measured:** `roomy_cache_size` reads **646 now**, against a boot value of **48** at 2026-10-15T12:00Z; the hourly series rises to that figure with small TTL-expiry dips (163→153 at 10-15T22, 472→438 at 10-17T04) and never approaches the 4,096 ceiling. But the per-NSID counters say the hit rate is dominated by one endpoint: `space.getMetadata` **14,894 hits / 32,075 misses / 3,702 evictions**, against `room.getMetadata` **552 / 3,770 / 2,001**, `getActivityFeed` **695 / 2,423 / 2,315**, `getThreads` **55 / 465 / 429**. The two routes that matter most for a reader's first paint — the room header and the activity feed — are **evicted nearly as often as they are filled**, because `inferSignals` emits a feed eviction for every `createMessage` anywhere (`cache/activityFeedCoverage.ts`).
  **Interpretation:** this is the cache doing what it was told (invalidation is the correctness mechanism; the TTL is the safety net) while the *shape* of the entries defeats it — a per-caller key over a per-space value means one writer's message invalidates every reader's copy of the same page.
- **Why now.** It is the substrate under proposals 2 and 3 and under the whole `db-slow` story: a miss is a full handler run, and the busiest handler run is the one that fans out per space. The counters that would have shown this have existed since the per-NSID breakdown landed; nobody has read them against the eviction reasons.
- **Scope sketch.** This is a design question, not a mechanical fix, so scope it as one: decide whether a hot read like the room header belongs in a per-space cache keyed by space rather than by caller (with the per-caller fields — `unreadCount`, `isMember`, read positions — layered at the response edge), or whether the feed's cross-space key needs a shorter TTL than the per-space pages it competes with. Record the decision in `cache/query-cache-plan.md` with the hit/miss/eviction table above; the code change follows from it.
- **Acceptance.** The per-NSID table in `/metrics` shows `getActivityFeed` and `room.getMetadata` with a hit rate approaching `getMetadata`'s, and `roomy_cache_evictions_total` falls; the decision is recorded either way, with the 24 h counters that justified it.
- **Suggested priority:** **P3** — nothing is incorrect, and the ceiling is not being hit; but two of the four hottest routes effectively have no cache, and that is the multiplier on every other performance item.
- **Not already tracked because.** `TASK-143` is write cost; nothing names `QueryCache`, `maxEntries`, `roomy_cache_nsid_*`, or the per-caller key. `cache/query-cache-plan.md` describes the invalidation model, not this hit-rate measurement.
