### 1. A bridged message delete rebuilds a room's whole activity projection inline, and that statement now takes up to 17 s

- **Evidence:** `db-slow`, production, 24 h — the rebuild statement
  (`with rooms(room_id) as (select value from json_each(?1)), entries as (…)`)
  ran **99 times, 140,151 ms of statement time, max 17,399 ms**, and **all 99
  runs are on one space**, `did:plc:qzie4v7qwnv3mzaflihrf56f` ("Crispy Toast
  Productions", a Discord-bridged space), every one on the `space-3` worker. The
  same signature read 75 runs / 42.4 s / max 3,490 ms on 2026-11-08 — the daily
  cost tripled and the worst case went 5× overnight. Anchors on this ref:
  `packages/appserver/src/materialization/deleteMessage.ts:44-46` calls
  `rebuildRoomActivity` as a delete side-effect on the write path, and
  `packages/appserver/src/queries/roomActivityProjection.ts:102-119`
  (`ENTRIES_BY_ROOM_SQL`) is the O(messages-in-room) scan it redoes;
  `:124` is `rebuildRoomActivitySql`. The write path pays it: the 6,329 ms
  `space.roomy.space.sendEvents` trace at 2026-11-09T06:53:53Z (`38fcf258…`)
  spends **6,325 ms in `sendEvents.write.streamManager`** on that space, and the
  6,295 ms rebuild statement on the same space ends at 06:53:59.895Z — the
  same instant the request returns. The deletes are the bridge's: a Discord
  message delete becomes a `space.roomy.message.deleteMessage.v0` event at
  `packages/discord-bridge/src/services/message-edit-delete.ts:232-240`, and
  the same space is the one the bridge logs ingest skips for 109 times a day.
- **Why now:** every delete in that room re-scans every message in it, and the
  per-space worker serialises, so a Discord delete storm makes ordinary chat on
  that space wait seconds — three sampled sends were ≥5 s in the same fifteen
  minutes as the rebuilds (06:49:11Z 5,112 ms, 06:53:53Z 6,329 ms, 07:00:16Z
  5,773 ms). The count is rising rather than steady, so whatever bounds it
  today will not next week.
- **Scope sketch:** make the delete path cost O(deleted) rather than
  O(messages-in-room): compute the room's new latest-message/recent-author
  summary from the index instead of rescanning (`idx_entities_room_sort`,
  `db/schema-space.sql:82`, and `idx_edges_label_head`, `:107`, answer the
  "newest by (ts, id)" half as a seek); or defer the rebuild to the maintenance
  pass and serve the scan meanwhile, so a write never waits on it. Do not
  change what the projection means — the tests in
  `queries/roomActivityProjection.test.ts` pin equality with the fallback.
- **Acceptance:** a single delete in a room with ≥10,000 messages does not
  produce a `db-slow` entry (i.e. stays under the fixed 50 ms threshold); a
  replay of 1,000 deletes in one room rebuilds at most once per room and stays
  under 1 s total; the projection still equals the live scan on the existing
  equality tests.
- **Suggested priority:** P2
- **Not already tracked because:** CF24 has carried this for two weeks as a
  steady 40 s/day problem with no task and no PR. Today's numbers are 3.5× the
  daily cost and 5× the worst statement, and the write-path trace now ties it to
  a user-visible send latency — that is the difference between a curiosity and a
  task.

### 2. A slow request cannot name the worker it waited on

- **Evidence:** 150 sampled production traces, 24 h. **8 of 95** `getMessages`
  and **6 of 52** `sendEvents` spans are ≥1 s. The slowest `getMessages`
  (`3bed13e2…`, 2026-11-09T07:00:16Z, room `01M0S1KSQ5E127KRZDDCJAKCP3`) is
  **5,371 ms of which `getMessages.requireRead` is 5,152 ms**; `selectMessages`
  is 77 ms and `prioritiseLinks` 141 ms. `sendEvents.access` peaks at **2,767 ms**
  on a 5,112 ms send. Yet the corresponding statement is *not* slow: the whole
  `select … from room_access` family peaks at **296 ms** in 24 h, and no
  `space-1` statement in that minute exceeds 5.5 s. So the seconds are spent
  queued, not executing — and nothing in the trace says so. Mean DB queue depth
  since boot is **5.28** on `readstate` (774,170 queries) and **1.38** on
  `space-2` (452,894), i.e. the backlog is structural, not a one-off stall.
  `roomy_db_wait_seconds` exists but is per-worker aggregate; no span carries
  it.
- **Why now:** proposals 1 and 3 and every prior latency record in this series
  end in "the time cannot be attributed". The appserver now has sub-spans at the
  handler level and a statement timer (TASK-461/`#598`, open); the missing third
  piece is the wait, which is the one that actually dominates these traces. One
  attribute converts "some handler is slow" into "this worker was backed up",
  which is the difference between tuning a query and fixing a pool.
- **Scope sketch:** in the DB worker, record the queue wait per request;
  in `withSpan`, set `roomy.db_worker` and `roomy.queue_wait_ms` on the spans
  that issue DB work (`getMessages.requireRead`, `sendEvents.access`,
  `selectMessages`). No behaviour change, no new metric — attributes on existing
  spans.
- **Acceptance:** a sampled slow `getMessages` trace names the worker and the
  milliseconds it spent waiting, and the two sum to its duration; on a quiet
  hour `roomy.queue_wait_ms` is under 10 ms on every span of the same route.
- **Suggested priority:** P3
- **Not already tracked because:** TASK-461/`#598` measures statement time and
  is open; CF25 is about client↔server trace propagation. Neither touches
  per-request queue wait, and no PR adds a span attribute anywhere.

### 3. The bridge drops 109 Discord messages a day as "no content and no attachments"

- **Evidence:** `discord-bridge`, `scope=ingest`, 24 h: **109** warn lines, all
  `Skipping message <id> in did:plc:qzie4v7qwnv3mzaflihrf56f: no content and no
  attachments`, ~4.5/hour, the newest at 2026-11-09T07:04:31Z. The channel is
  the same bridged space as proposal 1. The bridge already has an opinion about
  what counts as content (its warn names exactly two: text and attachments), so
  a Discord message whose payload is *only* an embed, a sticker, a poll or a
  system row is silently dropped, and the line is the only record.
- **Why now:** it is a one-line-per-message, permanent loss of bridged
  conversation, and nothing counts it — 109/day is roughly one message every
  thirteen minutes for a space whose users expect Discord parity. Cheapest of
  the three to scope and verify.
- **Scope sketch:** classify what was skipped and act on the classes: forward a
  message that has embeds as its first embed URL plus title, and count every
  skip in a `bridge_ingest_skipped_total{reason}` counter surfaced on the
  bridge's status API. Keep the current skip for anything genuinely contentless.
- **Acceptance:** the counter names each reason and sums to the skipped
  messages; a Discord message carrying only a link preview arrives in Roomy as
  a message containing that link; the existing ingest tests still pass.
- **Suggested priority:** P3
- **Not already tracked because:** no queue entry and no PR touches the ingest
  skip. `#590` is about the member-count lookup, `#503` about forum channels.
- **Honest limit:** the log names the message ids but not what they contained,
  so "embed-only" is the leading explanation, not an observed one. The first
  step of the task is to print the payload shape that was rejected.

## Honest limits

- Proposal 1's causal chain is: the rebuild statement is 6,295 ms on that space
  in the minute of a 6,329 ms send whose 6,325 ms is inside
  `write.streamManager`, and the delete side-effect is the only caller on the
  write path. It is an exact co-occurrence plus a source read, **not** a span
  that shows the rebuild inside that request — `sendEvents.write.streamManager`
  has no sub-spans. The 17,399 ms worst case is a `db-slow` statement time, so
  it excludes queue wait before the statement started.
- Proposal 2 says the seconds are queue wait because no statement matches them.
  The alternative — a statement below the 50 ms `db-slow` floor repeated dozens
  of times — is not excluded; that is exactly what the proposed attribute would
  settle.
- Proposal 3 knows what the bridge logged, not what Discord sent: the skip line
  carries the message id and nothing else, so "embed-only message" is the
  leading hypothesis.
- `db-slow` is a fixed 50 ms threshold, so every count here is a floor.
- The metrics quoted above are still the blended prod+staging series until
  `#620` lands and the staging collector is given `ALLOY_ENV` (CF27). Where a
  number could be affected by that, the live `/metrics` and `/health` reading is
  quoted instead of the Mimir series; the RSS trend uses Mimir's 6-hour maxima,
  which are the serving process only because it is the larger of the two.
- The client sample is the newest 2,000 records, not the full 24 h population
