### 1. The bridge permanently drops Discord→Roomy messages when the appserver is unreachable, and it dropped 223 of them yesterday

- **Evidence.** `Discord→Roomy send abandoned after re-offers` (scope `send-retry`, level `error`) — **470 lines in the last 24 h and 470 in 7 d**, i.e. the whole 7 d population is from one day; the 24 h before it has **0**. **223 distinct `discordId`s**, every one of them abandoned-only (0 appear in the `re-offer landed` set of 147 ids), across all four bridged spaces: `did:plc:qzie4v7qwnv3mzaflihrf56f` (427), `did:plc:4moccs43r5v2xzkynae3xk2u` (32), `did:plc:yhcspkk2ocsyoxkkb73emg64` (9), `did:plc:4r7sywony4sjxadtrjthov2w` (2). The `error` field is a Cloudflare 502 from `api.roomy.space` in **396 of 470** and a 404 in 69. The window is 2026-10-14T17:51Z → 21:0xZ, in 10-minute buckets of 12, 23, 3, 20, 7, 9, 2, 13, 5, 61, 31, 19, 27, 42, 69, 23, 14, 73, 17 — which is exactly the two windows the appserver spent down (`up{instance="appserver.railway.internal:8080"}` reads 0 from 17:15 to 18:45 and from 18:51 to 20:55; the log has no line at all between 17:13:37.783Z and 18:44:58.442Z, nor between 18:49:41.105Z and 20:57:28.993Z).
- **Why now.** These are real users' Discord messages that never reached Roomy and never will. The budget is 8 attempts — `SEND_SWEEP_MAX_ATTEMPTS = 8` with `SWEEP_BASE_MS = 30 s`, doubling, capped at 30 min — so roughly **91 minutes** of wall clock from first failure to terminal, against an outage of 1 h 31 m followed, 6 minutes after service returned, by 2 h 08 m. `getStaleFailedSends` selects `WHERE terminal = 0`, `markFailedSendTerminal` is a one-way door, and the only reader is `GET /sends/failed`, which lists and counts them. There is no path back: the entry's `event_json` is still there, the mapping is still there, and nothing ever offers it again. The `Backfill blocked` line at least says "the pair stays terminal until /roomy …"; a terminal send says nothing at all outside a log line and a REST listing nobody is polling.
- **Scope sketch.** Two halves, and the first is a one-line decision. (a) **Make the budget cover an outage rather than a blip**: raise `SEND_SWEEP_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (with the cap at 30 min the marginal cost of a 24 h budget is 48 extra attempts on an entry nobody is looking at) and, more importantly, stop counting attempts against wall clock rather than failures — the entries above already read `attempts: 11` and `12`, i.e. the counter is also being bumped by `enqueueFailedSend`'s `ON CONFLICT … attempts = failed_sends.attempts + 1`, so the budget is consumed twice per failure and the 91-minute figure is an over-estimate of what remains. (b) **Give terminal rows an operator path**: `failed_sends` keeps every field a replay needs (`event_json`, `mapping_kind`, `mapping_value`), so a `POST /sends/failed/replay` (or an admin slash command) that clears `terminal` and resets `next_retry_at` to now is the whole feature — the sweep already does the rest.
- **Acceptance.** With the appserver refusing `sendEvents` for 3 hours, an incoming Discord message arrives in Roomy once the appserver returns, and `countFailedSends().terminal` is 0. Asserted against the repository layer with a clock the test advances; the 502 body the bridge received is the fixture.
- **Suggested priority:** **P1** — permanent loss of user content, 223 messages in one afternoon, on the deployment serving now.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `failed_sends`, `send-retry`, or the bridge's retry budget. `TASK-22` is about embeds in bridged messages; CF7 is about thread *creates* being dropped on the backfill path, which `#406` fixes and which this PR's build does not carry. This is the message path, which `sendEventOrQueue` has been routing through the queue since before the deployed build — the queue works and the budget is too short.

### 2. The restart-rate signal the appserver documents is inert: 51 boots in 14 days and `roomy_process_starts_total` reads exactly 1 at every sample

- **Evidence.** `fatal.ts` exists to make a restart loop scrape-visible, and its own comment prescribes the query: `increase(roomy_process_starts_total[10m]) > 3`. **The series is `1` at all 19,643 samples Prometheus holds over 14 d**, `max_over_time(roomy_process_starts_total[14d])` is `1`, and `max_over_time(resets(roomy_process_starts_total[1h])[14d:1h])` is **0** — while the log records **51 `Appserver listening` lines in 14 d** and 17 in the last 8 d, four of them in the audited window alone (15:41:00, 18:44:58, 20:57:29 on `f1472c24`; 06:22:26 on `d093777d`). The mechanism is read on this ref: `recordProcessStart()` (`fatal.ts:60`) calls `processStarts.inc()` once per process, and `metrics.ts:74` gives every `counter()` call a *fresh* `CounterFamily` it installs with `families.set(name, fam)`. A process that restarts therefore scrapes `1` before and `1` after, and a Prometheus counter that returns to the same value produces neither a `resets()` nor an `increase()` — the two functions that could see it both look for a decrease or a delta across the window. The `fatal.test.ts` assertions all run inside one process, so the test that would catch this (`recordProcessStart bumps the series` builds its own `Metrics`, increments twice, and reads `2`) cannot: it never crosses a process boundary.
- **Why now.** This is why a 3.5-hour, two-window outage across 2026-10-14 was found by a colleague noticing traffic and stopping the appserver by hand, not by the stack. The only instrument that *did* see it is `up`, which is Prometheus's own and says nothing about restarts. The appserver has no alert rules of its own: Mimir exposes 40 rules in four groups — `asserts-node.rules`, `node-exporter`, `node-exporter-filesystem` and `node-exporter.rules` — 24 of them alerting, and **not one references a `roomy_*` series**. Four restarts of the same build in one day is the shape a deploy loop, an OOM kill and a crash loop all share, and nothing in the stack can currently tell them apart.
- **Scope sketch.** Emit a value that differs between processes instead of one that returns to the same value: a `roomy_process_boot_timestamp_seconds` gauge set from `Date.now()/1000` at boot, and/or `roomy_process_uptime_seconds` set from `process.uptime()` on each scrape. Either makes "this process is not the one I saw five minutes ago" a one-line PromQL expression (`changes(roomy_process_boot_timestamp_seconds[1h]) > 0`) and makes the restart visible without any new infrastructure. Keep `roomy_process_starts_total` for the in-process count it actually is. The `fatal.test.ts` case that would have caught this asserts across a process boundary — spawn the fixture in `fatal.fixture.ts` twice and read both `# TYPE` blocks.
- **Acceptance.** After a restart, `roomy_process_boot_timestamp_seconds` changes and `changes(...[1h])` is 1; a test drives two fixture boots and asserts the two renders differ. On the current build the same expression is 0 across 51 boots.
- **Suggested priority:** **P2** — no user-visible defect, but it is the difference between an outage that paged and an outage that was noticed by hand, and the fix is one gauge.
- **Not already tracked because.** Nothing in the queue names `fatal.ts`, `roomy_process_starts_total`, or process-level observability. `TASK-164` is the opposite direction (reconciling a task's *deploy* claim against `/health`), and `TASK-148`/`TASK-146` are about the coordinator's own loop.

## Limits of the evidence

- **Proposal 1's permanence is a source reading plus a set difference, not a replay.** What is exact: the 470 / 470 counts, the 223 distinct ids, the empty intersection with the 147 landed ids, the per-space and per-hour splits, the 502/404 split, the window, and the source read of `SEND_SWEEP_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, `sweepBackoffMs`, `markFailedSendTerminal`, `getStaleFailedSends` (`terminal = 0`) and the `ON CONFLICT … terminal = 0` in `enqueueFailedSend`. The claim is that the *restart-rate* signal the code documents is inert, not that nothing at all could see a restart.
